Analytics

NeonKey uses Umami (opens in new tab) for privacy-friendly analytics. Umami doesn't use cookies, doesn't collect personal data and doesn't track you across sites. It records anonymous page views and a few button clicks (for example newsletter signups and service enquiries) so I can see what's useful. Blocking the script won't break anything on the site.

Advertising

NeonKey runs ads on Google and uses Google Ads conversion tracking (the Google tag) to see whether those ads lead to visits and enquiries. The tag is loaded from Google on every page and may set cookies or collect device and interaction data, such as the pages you view and whether you send a message through the contact form, to measure ad performance. See how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services (opens in new tab). Blocking the script won't break anything on the site.

The same Google tag also runs Google Analytics, which measures visits and where they come from (for example search, ads or other sites) and may likewise use cookies and collect device and interaction data. The same Google page (opens in new tab) explains how Google uses this information.

On the newsletter signup page and its thank-you page only, NeonKey also loads the Meta (Facebook and Instagram) pixel and the Reddit pixel. They tell Meta and Reddit when someone who saw a NeonKey ad visits the signup page or subscribes, so those ads can be measured. They may set cookies and collect device and interaction data. They don't receive your email address. See Meta's privacy policy (opens in new tab) and Reddit's privacy policy (opens in new tab). Blocking them won't break anything on the site.

Newsletter

Unlock the Week is sent with Kit (opens in new tab) (formerly ConvertKit), which processes subscriber data on NeonKey's behalf. When you subscribe, your email address goes straight to Kit. Kit stores it along with basic sign-up and delivery data, such as when and where you signed up and whether an issue was delivered, opened or clicked.

Kit sends a confirmation email first, and you're only added once you click it. If you haven't confirmed after about a day, I send you one reminder through Resend (opens in new tab), NeonKey's email delivery provider, with a button to get a fresh confirmation link. That's the only reminder: if you don't confirm, you won't hear from me again, and the reminder itself has an unsubscribe link.

Your email is used only to send the newsletter and is never sold. Every issue has an unsubscribe link, and you can ask for your data to be deleted at any time by emailing hello@neonkey.tech.

Contact

The contact form is delivered by Web3Forms (opens in new tab), which passes your name, email, topic and message to hello@neonkey.tech. Messages, whether sent through the form or by email, are kept only as long as needed to reply and are never sold.

Payments

Online payments, such as the Email Lockdown checkout, the Monthly Remote Support Plan and invoices, are handled by Stripe (opens in new tab). Stripe collects your card details directly, and NeonKey never sees or stores your full card number. Stripe shares your name, email, business name (if you give one), what you bought and the amount with NeonKey so I can deliver the work, send receipts and handle refunds. Stripe keeps payment records as its own policy and the law require.

Email Lockdown

When you buy the Email Lockdown, Stripe passes the domain you type at checkout, along with your name, email and business name, to a small NeonKey service on Cloudflare Workers. It checks your domain's public DNS records (the same records anyone can look up) and emails your report through Resend (opens in new tab). The report is turned into a PDF with Cloudflare's browser rendering service, which processes the report itself, not any other data. No passwords are needed or collected.

Your order record (name, email, business name, domain and check results) is kept in Cloudflare for 180 days, so I can run your rechecks, answer questions and deal with any payment dispute. After that it deletes automatically. Copies of the emails sent to you, and order notices sent to NeonKey at hello@neonkey.tech, are kept in NeonKey's business email as business records. To have them deleted sooner, email hello@neonkey.tech (payment records Stripe must keep are the exception).

Client files and access

Some work needs files or access from you, such as a spreadsheet export, your FAQ or price list, or a helper invite to your domain or admin settings. I ask only for what the job needs, and I never ask for your passwords, card or bank details, or other sensitive personal information. If you send something like that by mistake, I delete it and let you know.

Files you send for a free report are deleted within 7 days after the report is sent. Files for paid work are deleted within 30 days after handoff, unless you ask me to keep them. Deleting means removing them from NeonKey's email (including the trash), any copies and any saved files. Client files are never shared or sold, and they're never pasted into the free Lab demos. At handoff, you remove my access (or I confirm it's removed), and every admin login stays with you.

AI inquiry triage demo (Lab)

The AI inquiry triage demo sends the text you type to a small NeonKey API running on Cloudflare Workers. The API forwards it to Google's Gemini API (opens in new tab) to generate the result, then returns the result to your browser. NeonKey doesn't store or log the message or the result.

The demo uses Gemini's free tier. Under Google's terms, content sent on the free tier may be used by Google to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. Please don't paste private, sensitive or personal information into the demo. Because of those terms, the demo isn't available in the UK, Switzerland or the EEA.

To enforce rate limits (5 runs a minute and 30 a day per visitor, plus a daily total), the API keeps short-lived counters in Cloudflare: a one-way hash of your IP address with the date, and a run count. The counters expire after 48 hours. Cloudflare, as the host, may also process standard request data such as your IP address; see Cloudflare's privacy policy (opens in new tab).

Other AI demos (Lab)

Three more Lab demos work the same way as the triage demo: what you send goes through a small NeonKey API on Cloudflare Workers to Google's Gemini API (opens in new tab) on the free tier, the result comes back to your browser, and NeonKey doesn't store or log what you send or the result. The same free-tier warning applies: please don't send private, sensitive or personal information, and these demos aren't available in the UK, Switzerland or the EEA.

  • The review reply demo sends the review text you type. It shares the triage demo's API and limits (5 runs a minute and 30 a day per visitor).
  • The Office AI demo sends the question you type (up to 400 characters); the documents it answers from are made-up samples. Limits: 5 runs a minute and 30 a day per visitor, plus a daily total.
  • The receipt sorter demo sends the receipt photo or PDF you choose (up to 5 MB). Limits: 5 runs a minute and 20 a day per visitor, plus a daily total.

Each API keeps the same kind of short-lived rate-limit counters as the triage demo: a one-way hash of your IP address with the date and a run count, deleted 48 hours after last use.

Service quiz

The "What service do I need?" quiz works out your result in your browser. Your answers aren't sent anywhere unless you ask for your plan by email.

If you do, your email address, optional first name and quiz answers are sent to NeonKey through a small API on Cloudflare Workers to prepare your plan. The plan is emailed to you once through Resend (opens in new tab), NeonKey's email delivery provider, and a copy with your email and answers is sent to NeonKey at hello@neonkey.tech so I can prepare and follow up if you reply. That copy is kept only as long as needed and is never sold. You're only added to the newsletter if you tick the newsletter box; then Kit sends you a confirmation email first (see Newsletter above).

To limit abuse, the API keeps short-lived counters in Cloudflare: a one-way hash of your IP address with the date, a one-way hash of your email address (so the same address can't be sent more than one plan a day), and a daily total. These expire after about 24 hours, and your answers aren't stored there. To have your quiz data deleted, email hello@neonkey.tech.

Free IT kit

If you ask for the free Starter IT Kit, your email address and anything optional you add (first name, business name, team size and biggest tech headache) are sent to NeonKey through the same small API on Cloudflare Workers as the service quiz. The download link is emailed to you once through Resend (opens in new tab), and a copy with those details is sent to NeonKey at hello@neonkey.tech so we can follow up about the kit. That copy is kept only as long as needed and is never sold. You're only added to the newsletter if you tick the newsletter box; then Kit sends you a confirmation email first. The same short-lived, hashed abuse counters described under Service quiz apply, kept separately for the kit. To have this data deleted, email hello@neonkey.tech.

Cookies

NeonKey itself sets no cookies, but the Google tag and, on the newsletter pages, the Meta and Reddit pixels (see Advertising above) may set cookies to measure ad performance. Links to other sites, such as retailers, lead to pages that may set their own cookies.

Hosting

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages (opens in new tab). Like any web host, GitHub may process standard request data such as your IP address to serve and secure the site.

Your choices
