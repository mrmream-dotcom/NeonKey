I Automated My Own Sales Log Before Offering It to Anyone

Nov42026
By Michael ReamWednesday, November 4, 20263 min readInsightsAutomation

Before I build an automation for anyone else, I want it running on my own business first. So the first one I built was for NeonKey.

It's a simple one: every sale now writes itself into the tracker I already use to follow up with people. Nobody copies anything.

What it does

  • A sale logs itself. When someone buys from NeonKey, the tracker gets who bought, what they bought and when.
  • It finds the right row. If that person was already in the tracker, their row is updated. If they're new, a row is added.
  • Refunds update the same row. If an order is refunded, that row changes to "refunded," so the numbers I review each week match what really happened.

Try it: a made-up sales log

Fictional customers. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Click "Simulate a sale" to watch a row update on its own.

Why I trust it with my own sales

  • It only believes real notices. The payment company signs every notice it sends. Anything that can't be verified is ignored, so nobody can fake a sale into the tracker.
  • Practice runs don't count. Test payments are skipped, so the log never looks better than reality.
  • I tested it with real money first. Before turning it on, I ran a live payment and a refund all the way through and checked the row changed both times.
  • A person still reads it. I look at the tracker every week. The automation does the typing. I still make the decisions.

It's new, so it hasn't handled a big pile of sales yet. That's exactly why it runs on my business first: if there's a rough edge, I find it before a client ever does.

What this could look like for you

The idea isn't limited to payments. Most jobs shaped like "something happened, write it down" can log themselves:

  • A web form is filled in
  • A booking is made or canceled
  • An invoice gets paid
  • A quote is accepted

It usually lands in a spreadsheet or tool you already use, so there's nothing new to learn. You keep every login, and you get written notes on what was set up.
