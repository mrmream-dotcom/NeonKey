Office AI, ask your own documents.

Every small business has the same few questions on repeat: "What do we charge on a Sunday?" "How do I book time off?" "What do I tell a customer who smells gas?" The answers are in a handbook or a price sheet somewhere. This demo lets you ask a made-up plumbing company's documents in everyday words. The AI answers only from those documents, shows the exact lines it used, and says so when the answer isn't there. It's the core of our AI Intake & FAQ Assistant, running for real.

Staff handbook (excerpt)5 sections · updated Sep 15, 2026

Office hours

The office is open Monday to Friday, 7:30 AM to 5:00 PM. Phones forward to the on-call technician outside those hours. The office is closed on New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day.

Requesting time off

Request paid time off in the scheduling app under Me > Time Off at least two weeks ahead. Requests for the week of Thanksgiving or the last two weeks of December need four weeks' notice. Kelly in the office approves requests in the order they come in. Full-time staff earn 10 days of paid time off per year, rising to 15 days after three years.

Calling in sick

If you are sick, call or text the office line at 555-0147 before 7:00 AM so jobs can be moved. Do not just message a coworker. More than two sick days in a row needs a quick note to Kelly when you are back.

Company vans

Company vans are for work use only, plus the drive between home and the first and last job of the day. No passengers who are not employees. Log fuel purchases with the fuel card and keep the receipt in the van folder. Report any damage or warning light to Kelly the same day.

Safety basics

Wear eye protection when cutting pipe or using a torch. Every van carries a first aid kit and a fire extinguisher; check both on the first Monday of each month. Report every injury, even a small one, to Kelly the same day.

Standard rates

Service call (first 30 minutes, includes diagnosis): $95. Labor after the first 30 minutes: $120 per hour, billed in 15-minute steps. Parts are billed at the price on the parts list.

After-hours and holidays

After-hours service call (weekdays after 5:00 PM, Saturdays and Sundays): $185, and labor is $180 per hour. Holiday service call (the six holidays the office is closed): $250, and labor is $200 per hour.

Common jobs (quote ranges)

Tank water heater replacement, 40 or 50 gallon: $1,450 to $2,100 installed, depending on fuel type and venting. Tankless water heater: quote only after a site visit. Toilet replacement with a standard toilet: $380 to $520 installed. Drain cleaning, single fixture: $150 to $250. Always say these are ranges and the final price comes from the technician on site.

Payment

Payment is due when the job is finished. We take cards, checks and bank transfer. No cash over $500. Jobs over $3,000 need a 30% deposit when the job is booked. We do not offer financing.

Discounts

Customers aged 65 and over get 10% off labor. The discount does not apply to parts or to after-hours and holiday rates. Do not offer any other discount without Kelly's OK.

Office how-tos5 sections · updated Oct 20, 2026

Booking a job

Open the scheduling app, choose New Job, and fill in the customer's name, address, phone number and a short description of the problem. Ask if there are pets, a gate code or parking limits and add them to the notes. Choose the first open slot that fits the job length: 1 hour for a service call, 4 hours for a water heater replacement. The app texts the customer a confirmation automatically.

Emergency calls

Burst pipes, no water to the whole house, sewage backing up and no heat when it is below freezing outside count as emergencies. Book them in the next open slot today, even if that means moving a routine job, and tell the customer the after-hours rate if it is after 5:00 PM. Tell the customer where their main water shutoff valve usually is: near the water heater, in the basement or crawl space, or by the street.

Gas smell

If a customer says they smell gas, tell them to leave the building right away, not to flip any light switches or use anything that could make a spark, and to call the gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside. Do not book a regular appointment first. Once the gas utility says the home is safe, we can book a visit.

On-call rotation

One technician is on call each week from Friday 5:00 PM to the next Friday 5:00 PM. The rotation is posted in the scheduling app under Team > On Call. To swap a week, both technicians agree and tell Kelly by Wednesday.

Leaving a job

Before leaving, run the water, check for leaks, take a photo of the finished work and upload it to the job in the app. Collect payment and mark the job Complete. If the customer is not home, leave the invoice in the app and text them that it was sent.

Warranty and refund policy4 sections · updated Aug 1, 2026

Labor warranty

Our labor is covered for 1 year from the job date. If the same problem comes back because of our work, we fix it at no charge for labor.

Parts and equipment

Parts and equipment carry the manufacturer's warranty. Water heaters usually come with a 6 to 12 year tank warranty from the maker; check the model's paperwork. We register new water heaters with the maker for the customer.

Refunds

Service call fees are not refundable, because the visit and diagnosis already happened. If a customer is unhappy with a repair, book a free return visit within 30 days first. Any refund needs Kelly's approval.

What the warranty does not cover

Damage from freezing, misuse, or work done by someone else after our visit. Parts the customer supplied themselves are not covered.

What it does

One question goes in. One checked answer comes out:

  • An answer in everyday words, 1 to 4 sentences, with prices, times and names exactly as the documents state them.
  • A coverage label: answered from the docs, partly answered, or not in the docs. "Not in the docs" is a feature, not a failure. It's the AI admitting it doesn't know instead of making something up.
  • Sources: up to three word-for-word quotes, each with the document and section it came from. Tap one and the page opens that document and highlights the section, so you can check it yourself in two seconds.
  • Who to ask next when the documents only partly cover it, for example "Check with Kelly in the office."
  • A confidence level, which the checker lowers automatically if any quote fails the check (more on that below).

Why a small business would want it

  • The owner stops being the help desk. In a small team, the same five questions land on one person all day. Most of them are already answered in a document nobody opens.
  • Faster, more consistent answers. A new hire gets the same answer about after-hours rates as a ten-year veteran, straight from the price sheet, not from memory.
  • Safety answers are always on hand. "Customer smells gas" should get the same correct script every time, at 7 PM on a Friday too.
  • It shows you what's missing. Every "not in the docs" answer is a gap in your documents. That's a to-do list for your handbook.

It won't replace a manager's judgment. It gets people to the right line in the right document, fast, and it's honest when that line doesn't exist.

How it works

  1. Question: this page sends your question (max 400 characters) to a small API.
  2. Worker: a Cloudflare Worker checks where the request came from (only neonkey.tech is allowed), applies the rate limits, and rejects anything empty or too long.
  3. Documents + question to the AI: the Worker sends all four documents plus your question to Google's Gemini model, with strict instructions: answer only from the documents, quote them word for word, and say "not in the docs" rather than guess. A JSON schema fixes the shape of the answer, and your question is treated as data, so instructions hidden in it are ignored.
  4. Quote check: before anything comes back, the Worker checks every quote against the real document text (next section).
  5. Answer: you see the answer, the coverage label and only the quotes that passed. Nothing is saved.

Why send all the documents every time? Because they're small: about 4,700 characters fits easily in one request. Bigger collections (a 200-page manual, years of policies) don't fit, so real tools first search the documents for the most relevant passages and send only those. That search step is called retrieval, and it's what tools like AnythingLLM do for you (see below).

Stack & tools

  • Cloudflare Workers (free plan)

Checking its work: the quote checker

The biggest risk with a document assistant is a confident answer that cites something the document never said. So the AI doesn't get the last word:

  • Every quote is matched against the real text. The checker ignores only capital letters, curly quotes, dashes and extra spaces. Any other difference and the quote is dropped.
  • Very short quotes don't count. A quote under 12 characters can't prove anything, so it's dropped too.
  • Dropped quotes lower the confidence. If any quote fails, the answer is marked low confidence and the page says how many were removed.
  • No checked quote, no trust. If the AI says the documents answer the question but none of its quotes pass, the answer gets a clear "Unverified, check the documents yourself" warning.

In testing, I fed the checker a made-up quote ("Sunday calls are free of charge for loyal customers") next to two real ones. It kept the two real quotes, dropped the fake one and lowered the confidence. A fake "we offer financing" quote on its own was marked unverified. The checker is plain code, not AI, so it behaves the same way every time.

What it can't catch: an answer whose quotes are real but whose summary still gets something wrong. That's why the sources are on screen and one tap away.

Where AnythingLLM fits

This demo is a small build of my own so that it can run safely on a public web page. For a real business with real documents, I'd usually start with AnythingLLM (opens in new tab), a free, open-source app from Mintplex Labs (MIT license) built for exactly this job:

  • Drop in your files: PDFs, Word documents, text files and more go into a "workspace," and it handles the retrieval step for bigger collections.
  • Runs where you choose: there's a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux, and a Docker version for a small server, which adds multiple user accounts.
  • Pick your AI: it can use a cloud model like Gemini or OpenAI on a paid tier, or a model running on your own computer, so the documents never leave the office.
  • Answers show their sources, like this demo does.

Which setup fits depends on how sensitive the documents are, how many people need it and what computer it would run on. We work that out before the quote. To be clear about where this Lab stands: the live demo above is the custom build, and I haven't published a hands-on AnythingLLM test yet. That's the next entry for this build, using the same four Birchline documents so the results can be compared side by side.

What it costs to run

For this demo, nothing so far. Each piece runs on a free tier, and each has limits:

  • Cloudflare Workers, free plan: 100,000 requests a day. The rate-limit counters live in a small Durable Object that's also included on the free plan. The 150-questions-a-day cap mainly protects the AI quota.
  • Google Gemini API, free tier: Google sets per-model request limits on the free tier and changes them from time to time. The trade-off is data use: on the free tier Google may use what's submitted to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. Google's terms also don't allow free-tier apps for users in the UK, Switzerland or the EEA, so the demo is switched off there. Client builds are different: they run on a paid API account in the client's own name, under the paid tier's data terms, not on this free sandbox.
  • Size of a question: the example runs below used about 1806–1974 tokens each (instructions + all four documents + question + answer), as reported by the API.

For a real business, I'd use a paid AI tier, where Google says prompts and responses aren't used to improve its products, or a local model through AnythingLLM. Paid tiers bill per token at Google's current rates (opens in new tab); for a small team asking short questions the bill is usually small, but it depends on volume and document size, so I'd estimate it with you during scoping. I won't promise you a number here.

Limitations

  • It's only as good as the documents. Out-of-date prices in the price sheet mean out-of-date answers. Someone has to own the documents.
  • The AI can still misread. The quote checker proves a quote is real, not that the answer read it correctly. Treat answers as a fast pointer to the source, and check anything that matters.
  • It's not for customers as-is. This demo answers staff questions. A customer-facing assistant needs extra rules about what it must never say (exact quotes, guarantees, medical or legal advice) and a handoff to a person. That's what the full AI Intake & FAQ Assistant setup adds.
  • Four small documents only. A larger set needs a retrieval step, which brings its own tuning (and its own ways to miss the right passage).
  • Free tiers can run out or slow down. If the main model is busy, the Worker retries on a lighter model. If both are unavailable, you get a friendly error instead of an answer.
  • Rate limits are per connection, not per person. People on the same office network, mobile carrier or VPN share one limit. The daily total cap is the backstop.

Build notes: decisions and what I changed

  • Reused what already worked. The Worker uses the same origin check, region block and Durable Object rate limiter as the AI inquiry triage demo. That limiter replaced an earlier one that let bursts through, so there was no reason to rebuild it.
  • No retrieval step, on purpose. With four short documents, sending everything is simpler and can't miss a passage. The page shows the documents in full so you can see exactly what the AI had.
  • Quotes, not just section names. Having the AI name the section it used would be simpler, but a section name can't be checked; an exact quote can. So the AI must quote, and code checks the quote.
  • The checker fixes mislabeled sources. If a quote is real but the AI names the wrong section, the checker finds the section the quote actually comes from and shows that one instead.
  • A question it can't answer is part of the samples. "Do we let staff work from home?" is deliberately not covered, so every visitor can see the assistant say no instead of inventing a remote-work policy.
  • What the first real runs showed. In the captured answers above, the Christmas time-off question came back "partly answered": the AI wasn't sure the week before Christmas counts as part of "the last two weeks of December," so it quoted the rule and sent the person to Kelly. A human would probably read it as covered. Cautious beats confidently wrong, but it's a good example of why the sources sit right next to the answer.
  • Made-up business, real-looking documents. Birchline Plumbing & Heating, its prices and "Kelly in the office" are fictional, with 555-01xx phone numbers that are reserved for fiction. The gas-smell steps follow standard public safety advice (leave, no switches, call from outside).

The Worker source lives in the site's repository under workers/office-ai/, with no secrets in it. The API key is stored as an encrypted Worker secret.
