Review reply helper, working demo.

Paste a Google, Yelp or Facebook review. An AI reads the mood, flags anything risky, tells you how fast to respond and drafts a public reply. You still read it, fix it and post it yourself. It's one of the builds on the "What can we automate?" menu, running for real.

What it does

One review goes in. One structured result comes out:

  • Mood: positive, mixed or negative, read from the words, not only the stars. A 4-star review can still hide a real complaint.
  • Topics: up to four short tags, like "wait time" or "friendly staff", so you can spot patterns across reviews.
  • Flags: a warning when the review names a staff member, mentions private or health details, raises a legal or safety concern, involves money, or looks fake or off-topic.
  • How fast to respond: high, medium or low, with a one-line reason.
  • A private tip for you, and a draft public reply you can edit. Anything the AI can't know is left as a [placeholder].

Why a small business would want it

  • Replies get written. Most owners mean to answer reviews and run out of time. A draft makes it a two-minute job.
  • The angry ones get seen first. A 1-star review about money gets flagged high, so it doesn't sit for a week.
  • Fewer replies you regret. The draft stays calm, doesn't argue and doesn't repeat private details, which is easy to forget when a review stings.

How it works

  1. Form: this page sends the review (max 2,000 characters), the star rating and the business name to a small API.
  2. Worker: the same Cloudflare Worker that runs the triage demo checks where the request came from, applies the rate limits and rejects anything empty or too long.
  3. AI: the Worker sends the review to Google's Gemini model with instructions and a strict JSON schema, so the answer always has the same shape. The review is treated as data, so instructions hidden inside it are ignored.
  4. Human review: you read the draft, fix it and post it yourself. In a real setup, new reviews would land in a shared inbox or a spreadsheet with the draft attached, never posted automatically.

Stack & tools

  • Astro (static page)
  • Cloudflare Workers (free plan)
  • Durable Objects (rate-limit counters only)
  • Google Gemini API (free tier)
  • JSON-schema structured output

Reply rules built into the prompt

  • Thank the reviewer and mention one specific thing they said.
  • On a bad review: apologize for their experience without admitting legal fault, don't argue, and invite them to continue privately.
  • Never repeat private or health details in public, and never use a full name.
  • Never offer discounts, refunds or gifts in exchange for a review or an edit. Review sites ban that.
  • Never invent facts, policies or fixes. Placeholders mark what you need to fill in.

What it costs to run

For this demo, nothing so far: it runs on the Cloudflare Workers free plan and the Gemini API free tier, with the same limits described on the triage demo page. For a real business, a paid AI tier is the better choice, because on the paid tier Google says it doesn't use prompts or responses to improve its products. A business gets a handful of reviews a week, so the AI bill is usually small, but it depends on volume, so it gets checked during scoping rather than promised here.

Limitations

  • The AI can misread tone. Sarcasm and mixed reviews are hard. Treat the mood and flags as a suggestion.
  • A human posts every reply. The draft doesn't know what actually happened, your policies or your schedule.
  • It doesn't connect to your review sites here. This demo works on pasted text. A client build would pull new reviews in automatically where the platform allows it.
  • Free tiers can run out or slow down. If the main model is busy, the Worker retries on a lighter model, and if both fail you get a friendly error.
