Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight

This domain covers the business side of security: who decides, how risk is measured and handled, how vendors are checked, which rules apply, and how you build good habits across an organization. It’s about 20% of SY0-701.

Exam version: these notes follow SY0-701 (V7). CompTIA plans to launch SY0-801 (V8) on or around November 17, 2026. SY0-701 stays available in English until June 11, 2027. Book the version you studied for.

What changes in V8 for this domain: Weight drops to about 14%. CompTIA's V8 summary lists the same six themes: governance, risk, third parties, compliance and privacy, audits, and awareness.

Governance

  • Policy: the high-level rule (for example, “sensitive data must be encrypted”).
  • Standard: the required specifics (which algorithms are approved).
  • Procedure: the step-by-step instructions (how to turn encryption on).
  • Guideline: recommended, but not required.

Governance also defines who owns what (data owners, custodians), how often documents are reviewed, and how exceptions are handled.

Risk management

Risk is likelihood × impact. You can avoid, mitigate, transfer (for example, with insurance), or accept a risk. Record decisions in a risk register, with an owner for each risk. Risk appetite is how much risk leadership is willing to take on.

Quick math:

  • SLE (single loss expectancy) = asset value × exposure factor
  • ALE (annualized loss expectancy) = SLE × ARO (annualized rate of occurrence)

So a $20,000 loss expected every other year (ARO 0.5) gives an ALE of $10,000. These are estimates, not forecasts. A business impact analysis (BIA) identifies what’s critical and how long it can be down.

Third-party risk

A vendor’s security becomes part of yours. Do due diligence before signing, and keep checking afterward. Useful contract terms include SLAs (service targets), NDAs (confidentiality), right to audit, breach-notification timelines, and an exit plan that covers how data is returned or destroyed.

Compliance and privacy

Being compliant isn’t the same as being secure. Know which rules actually apply to you:

  • PCI DSS if you handle payment cards.
  • HIPAA for covered US health information.
  • GDPR for personal data of people in the EU, within its scope.

Data minimization (collect only what you need) and clear retention schedules reduce your exposure.

Audits and assessments

  • An audit checks evidence against defined criteria. It can be internal or done by an independent outside party.
  • A vulnerability assessment finds and ranks weaknesses.
  • A penetration test actually tries to exploit them, with permission.
  • Attestation is a formal statement about controls. Every finding needs an owner and a fix-by date.

Security awareness

Training works best when it fits people’s roles and focuses on real behavior: spotting phishing, reporting quickly, and handling data safely. Measure reporting rates and speed, not just course completion. Phishing simulations should teach, not shame.

Key terms

Try to define each term before you tap it.

Policy

High-level mandatory organizational rule

Standard

Mandatory detailed requirement supporting policy

Procedure

Step-by-step instructions for an activity

Guideline

Recommended, generally nonmandatory advice

Risk appetite

Amount and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or retain

Data owner

Accountable for classification and authorized use of data

SLE

Expected monetary loss from one event

ARO

Expected frequency of an event per year

ALE

Annualized loss expectancy: SLE × ARO

Mitigate

Lower likelihood or impact with controls

Transfer

Shift some financial impact by contract or insurance

Accept

Explicitly retain a risk after informed approval

BIA

Business impact analysis of disruption and recovery priorities

Due diligence

Pre-contract examination of a supplier’s capabilities and risks

SLA

Contractual service-level targets and remedies

NDA

Confidentiality agreement between parties

Right to audit

Contract term allowing defined assurance activities

Supply chain risk

Exposure introduced by upstream vendors and components

Vendor offboarding

Revocation of access and recovery or destruction of data

PCI DSS

Industry security requirements for environments handling payment card data

HIPAA

US legal framework with privacy and security obligations for covered health data

GDPR

EU data-protection regulation with extraterritorial scope in some situations

Data minimization

Collect and retain only needed personal information

Retention schedule

Rules defining how long records must be kept

Regulatory compliance

Conformity to applicable legal and regulatory requirements

Internal audit

Review conducted by an organization’s own independent audit function

External audit

Review performed by an outside independent party

Penetration test

Authorized exercise attempting to demonstrate exploitability

Vulnerability assessment

Identification and ranking of vulnerabilities

Attestation

Formal declaration about controls or compliance

Remediation plan

Assigned corrective actions with deadlines and evidence

Phishing simulation

Controlled test of response to deceptive messages

Tabletop exercise

Discussion-based rehearsal of incident scenarios

Role-based training

Security instruction tailored to job responsibilities

Reporting culture

Environment encouraging quick reporting without unnecessary blame

Social engineering

Manipulating people into unsafe actions or disclosure

Security champion

Team member supporting good security practices locally

Quick self-check

6 practice questions in our own words. Pick an answer to see the explanation. Practice only: it doesn't predict exam results.

  1. Which document usually gives step-by-step instructions for following a policy? Obj. 5.1

    Show answer

    A. Procedure. Policies set the rule, standards set required specifics, guidelines suggest, and procedures give the steps.

  2. A loss event costs $20,000 each time and is expected 0.5 times per year. What is the ALE? Obj. 5.2

    Show answer

    B. $10,000. ALE = SLE x ARO = $20,000 x 0.5 = $10,000.

  3. A new supplier will store your employee data. What should happen first? Obj. 5.3

    Show answer

    A. Security due diligence and contract terms. Assess the vendor and agree on contract terms (notification, right to audit, data handling) before sharing sensitive data.

  4. A company wants to reduce its privacy exposure. Which practice fits best? Obj. 5.4

    Show answer

    A. Data minimization. Collecting and keeping only the personal data you actually need reduces what can be exposed.

  5. An authorized test actually exploits weaknesses to show what an attacker could do. What is it? Obj. 5.5

    Show answer

    A. Penetration test. A penetration test tries controlled exploitation. A vulnerability scan only finds and ranks weaknesses.

  6. Besides course completion rates, what best shows a phishing-awareness program is working? Obj. 5.6

    Show answer

    A. More people reporting suspicious messages, and reporting faster. Behavior is the real measure. More reports, faster, and fewer repeat clicks show the training is changing what people do.
