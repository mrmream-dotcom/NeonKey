<p class="lead">Whether it's a nephew, a freelancer or a company, whoever helps with your office tech needs access to do the job. The safe way to give it is not your password. Here's what to give them instead, and the red flags worth spotting before you hand over the keys.</p>

## Give them these 4 things instead of your password

### 1. Their own admin login

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace both let you add a separate admin account for a helper (it may cost one extra user license). With their own login, you can see who did what, and you can remove them cleanly later. If you share your password, you can't do either.

### 2. Accounts in your name

Your domain, your email and your backups should belong to your business, not to your helper's personal account. Whoever owns the domain controls your website and your email. If they leave, those can go with them.

### 3. Notes on every change

What changed, where, and how to undo it. In writing, every time. You don't need to understand every detail, but someone else should be able to pick up where your helper left off.

### 4. A one-minute exit

When the job ends, you should be able to remove their access yourself: suspend their account or remove their admin role. Ask them to show you how before they start.

## 5 red flags in small-business tech help

1. **"Just send me your password, it's quicker."** Instead: they get their own admin login.
2. **"I registered the domain under my account, easier that way."** Instead: it's registered to your business.
3. **"You don't need to know how it works."** You don't need every detail. You do need short notes on what was changed.
4. **New software before questions.** Good help asks how you work today before it sells you anything.
5. **"Only I can fix that."** Anything set up for you should be written down well enough that someone else could take over.

## What good tech help looks like

- **Jargon second, people first.** Terms explained in one line.
- **The problem before the tool.** If an app you already pay for, or an off-the-shelf one, does the job, they say so.
- **No promised results.** A clear scope and clear notes instead.
- **You keep every admin login** and get the notes and logins at the end.
