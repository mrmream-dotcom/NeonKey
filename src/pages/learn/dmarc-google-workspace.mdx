<p class="lead">If your business email runs on Google Workspace (you sign in to Gmail with you@yourbusiness.com), these three records help your email land in the inbox and make your address harder to fake. You add them where your domain's DNS is managed: often the company you bought the domain from (like GoDaddy, Namecheap or Squarespace) or Cloudflare.</p>

Not sure what you have now? <a href={siteUrl('#check-your-domain')} data-umami-event="learn-jump-check" data-umami-event-location="dmarc-google-workspace">Run the free check first</a>.

## Before you start

- You need **admin** access to the Google Admin console (admin.google.com).
- You need to be able to edit **DNS records** for your domain. Find out where your DNS is managed: it's the company your domain's "nameservers" point to.
- Make a note of every other tool that sends email as your domain (website forms, invoicing, newsletters). They may need their own entries later.

## Step 1: SPF

<ol class="steps">
  <li>In your DNS host, look for an existing TXT record that starts with <code>v=spf1</code>. You should only ever have <strong>one</strong>.</li>
  <li>If there's none, add a <strong>TXT</strong> record. Host or name: <code>@</code> (some hosts want it blank). Value: <code>v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all</code></li>
  <li>If one already exists, don't add a second. Edit the existing one and add <code>include:_spf.google.com</code> before the <code>~all</code> or <code>-all</code> at the end.</li>
</ol>

## Step 2: DKIM

<ol class="steps">
  <li>In the Admin console, go to <strong>Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email</strong>.</li>
  <li>Pick your domain and click <strong>Generate new record</strong>. Choose a 2048-bit key if your DNS host allows it. Keep the prefix selector as <code>google</code>.</li>
  <li>Google shows a host name (<code>google._domainkey</code>) and a long TXT value. Add that as a new <strong>TXT</strong> record in your DNS host, copied exactly.</li>
  <li>Wait for it to show up (often minutes, sometimes up to 48 hours), then go back to the Admin console and click <strong>Start authentication</strong>.</li>
</ol>

## Step 3: DMARC

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Add a <strong>TXT</strong> record. Host or name: <code>_dmarc</code>. Value: <code>v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourbusiness.com</code> (use an address you actually read).</li>
  <li><code>p=none</code> means "watch only": nothing gets blocked while you check that all your real email passes. The reports to that address show who is sending as your domain.</li>
  <li>After a few weeks of clean reports, change it to <code>p=quarantine</code> (failing email goes to spam), and later <code>p=reject</code> if you want to block fakes outright.</li>
</ol>

## Step 4: Check it worked

Run the free check below. You want SPF and DMARC to pass, and a DKIM key found at the <code>google</code> selector. Then send a test email to a Gmail address, open it, choose <strong>Show original</strong>, and look for SPF, DKIM and DMARC all showing <strong>PASS</strong>.

## Common mistakes

- **Two SPF records.** Merge them into one.
- **Forgetting other senders.** If your website or invoicing app sends as you, add its SPF include or DKIM record too, before you tighten DMARC.
- **Jumping straight to p=reject.** Start at <code>p=none</code> so you don't block your own email by accident.
- **Typos in long DKIM values.** Copy and paste; don't retype.
