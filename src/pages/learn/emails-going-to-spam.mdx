## 1. No SPF record

SPF is a public list, saved on your domain, of the services allowed to send email as you. Without it, Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook can't tell your real email from a fake, so they're more likely to filter it. You need exactly **one** SPF record (a TXT record starting with `v=spf1`). Two SPF records count as an error.

## 2. No DKIM signature

DKIM adds a digital signature to every email you send, using a key published on your domain. Receivers check the signature to confirm the email really came from you and wasn't changed on the way. You turn it on in your email provider's admin settings (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365), then add the record it gives you.

## 3. No DMARC policy

DMARC tells receiving servers what to do with email that fails SPF and DKIM, and sends you reports about who is sending as your domain. Since 2024, Google and Yahoo require it from bulk senders, and Google recommends SPF, DKIM and DMARC for everyone ([Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Our step-by-step guide covers <a href={siteUrl('/learn/dmarc-google-workspace/')}>setting up DMARC for Google Workspace</a>.

## 4. A tool sends for you, but isn't in your SPF

Invoicing apps, website forms, booking tools and newsletter services often send email "from" your address. If one of them isn't included in your SPF record (or doesn't sign with DKIM for your domain), its emails can fail checks even when your normal mailbox is fine. List every tool that sends as you, and check each one's help page for the SPF "include" or DKIM record it needs.

## 5. A brand-new domain or mailbox

New domains have no sending history, so filters are cautious at first. Start with normal one-to-one email to people who expect it, and avoid sending to a big list from a new domain in its first weeks.

## 6. What's in the email

Spam filters look at the message too. Common triggers:

- link shorteners, or lots of links,
- an email that's mostly one big image,
- attachments people didn't expect, especially zipped files,
- ALL CAPS subject lines and "act now" language.

A short message with one clear link to your own website is the safest shape.

## 7. People marking you as spam, or bounced addresses

If people don't recognize the sender, some hit "Report spam", and that teaches filters to block you. Sending to addresses that bounce hurts too. Only email people who asked to hear from you or are expecting a reply, and remove addresses that bounce.

## The quick order to fix things

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Run the free check below to see SPF, DKIM and DMARC for your domain.</li>
  <li>Fix anything marked <strong>Fix</strong> first: missing or duplicate SPF, no DMARC.</li>
  <li>Turn on DKIM in your email provider and in every tool that sends as you.</li>
  <li>Send yourself a test at a Gmail address and an Outlook address, and check where it lands.</li>
</ol>

Passing these checks doesn't promise the inbox, since content, sending history and each provider's own filters matter too. It does remove the most common setup reasons small-business email gets filtered.
