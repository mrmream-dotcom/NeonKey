<p class="lead">If your business email runs on Microsoft 365 (you sign in to Outlook with you@yourbusiness.com), these three records help your email land in the inbox and make your address harder to fake. You add them where your domain's DNS is managed, like GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace or Cloudflare.</p>

Not sure what you have now? <a href={siteUrl('#check-your-domain')} data-umami-event="learn-jump-check" data-umami-event-location="microsoft-365">Run the free check first</a>.

## Before you start

- You need admin access to Microsoft 365 (the Microsoft 365 admin center and the Microsoft Defender portal).
- You need to be able to edit DNS records for your domain.
- List every other tool that sends email as your domain (website forms, invoicing, newsletters). They may need their own entries.

## Step 1: SPF

<ol class="steps">
  <li>In your DNS host, look for an existing TXT record that starts with <code>v=spf1</code>. You should only have <strong>one</strong>.</li>
  <li>If there's none, add a <strong>TXT</strong> record. Host or name: <code>@</code> (or blank). Value: <code>v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all</code></li>
  <li>If one already exists, edit it and add <code>include:spf.protection.outlook.com</code> before the <code>~all</code> or <code>-all</code> at the end. Don't add a second record.</li>
</ol>

## Step 2: DKIM

Microsoft 365 uses two DKIM records, both CNAMEs, named <code>selector1._domainkey</code> and <code>selector2._domainkey</code>.

<ol class="steps">
  <li>In the Microsoft Defender portal, go to <strong>Email &amp; collaboration → Policies &amp; rules → Threat policies → Email authentication settings → DKIM</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select your domain. Microsoft shows the two CNAME records with the exact values to use for your domain.</li>
  <li>Add both as <strong>CNAME</strong> records in your DNS host, copied exactly.</li>
  <li>Once they show up (often minutes, sometimes longer), go back and turn <strong>Sign messages for this domain with DKIM signatures</strong> on.</li>
</ol>

## Step 3: DMARC

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Add a <strong>TXT</strong> record. Host or name: <code>_dmarc</code>. Value: <code>v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourbusiness.com</code> (an address you actually read).</li>
  <li>Leave it at <code>p=none</code> for a few weeks while you confirm all your real email passes.</li>
  <li>Then move to <code>p=quarantine</code>, and later <code>p=reject</code> if you want fakes blocked outright.</li>
</ol>

## Step 4: Check it worked

Run the free check below. You want SPF and DMARC to pass and a DKIM key found at <code>selector1</code> or <code>selector2</code>. Then send a test to a Gmail address, open it, choose <strong>Show original</strong>, and look for SPF, DKIM and DMARC all showing <strong>PASS</strong>.

## Common mistakes

- **Two SPF records.** Merge them into one.
- **DKIM records added but signing never turned on.** Step 2.4 is easy to miss.
- **Other senders left out.** Add their SPF include or DKIM records before you tighten DMARC.
- **Starting at p=reject.** Start with <code>p=none</code>.
