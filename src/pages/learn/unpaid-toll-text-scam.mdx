## What the text usually looks like

- "FINAL NOTICE" or "Toll Services" with a small balance, often under $10.
- A threat: a large late fee, a suspended license or registration, or "legal action".
- A link to a web address that is close to your toll agency's name, but not its real site.
- It arrives even if you haven't driven on a toll road lately, or don't have a toll account at all.

## The tells

1. **Pressure over a small amount.** A real toll agency mails notices and gives you time. A same-day threat over a few dollars is there to stop you from checking.
2. **The web address.** Scam links often stuff the agency's name into a different address, add extra words with dashes, or end in something unusual. Your agency's real site is the one you find yourself.
3. **The small fee is the hook.** The real goal is your card number, and sometimes your name, address and date of birth, which can be used for more fraud later.

## How to check your real balance

1. **Don't tap the link and don't reply.** Replying tells scammers your number is real.
2. **Open your toll account the usual way:** the agency's app, or its website typed in yourself or found through a search.
3. **Or call the number** printed on your toll statement or on the agency's own website.
4. **No account and no toll roads lately?** Then you can delete the text after reporting it.

## Report it, then delete it

- Forward the text to **7726** (it spells SPAM), so your carrier can block similar messages.
- Report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center also takes reports at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/).
- Then delete it.

## Already paid or typed your details in?

1. **Call your card issuer or bank now,** using the number on the back of your card or on the bank's own website. Ask them to stop the charge and replace the card.
2. **Watch your statements** for charges you don't recognize over the next few weeks.
3. **If you entered your date of birth, address or Social Security number,** follow the steps at [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/), and think about a free credit freeze with the three credit bureaus.
4. **Expect follow-up scams.** People who paid once often get "refund" or "fraud department" calls next. Hang up and call the number you find yourself.
