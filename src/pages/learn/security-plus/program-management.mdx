## Governance

- **Policy:** the high-level rule (for example, "sensitive data must be encrypted").
- **Standard:** the required specifics (which algorithms are approved).
- **Procedure:** the step-by-step instructions (how to turn encryption on).
- **Guideline:** recommended, but not required.

Governance also defines who owns what (data owners, custodians), how often documents are reviewed, and how exceptions are handled.

## Risk management

Risk is **likelihood × impact**. You can **avoid**, **mitigate**, **transfer** (for example, with insurance), or **accept** a risk. Record decisions in a **risk register**, with an owner for each risk. **Risk appetite** is how much risk leadership is willing to take on.

Quick math:

- **SLE** (single loss expectancy) = asset value × exposure factor
- **ALE** (annualized loss expectancy) = SLE × **ARO** (annualized rate of occurrence)

So a $20,000 loss expected every other year (ARO 0.5) gives an ALE of $10,000. These are estimates, not forecasts. A **business impact analysis (BIA)** identifies what's critical and how long it can be down.

## Third-party risk

A vendor's security becomes part of yours. Do **due diligence** before signing, and keep checking afterward. Useful contract terms include **SLAs** (service targets), **NDAs** (confidentiality), **right to audit**, breach-notification timelines, and an exit plan that covers how data is returned or destroyed.

## Compliance and privacy

Being compliant isn't the same as being secure. Know which rules actually apply to you:

- **PCI DSS** if you handle payment cards.
- **HIPAA** for covered US health information.
- **GDPR** for personal data of people in the EU, within its scope.

**Data minimization** (collect only what you need) and clear **retention schedules** reduce your exposure.

## Audits and assessments

- An **audit** checks evidence against defined criteria. It can be internal or done by an independent outside party.
- A **vulnerability assessment** finds and ranks weaknesses.
- A **penetration test** actually tries to exploit them, with permission.
- **Attestation** is a formal statement about controls. Every finding needs an owner and a fix-by date.

## Security awareness

Training works best when it fits people's roles and focuses on real behavior: spotting phishing, reporting quickly, and handling data safely. Measure **reporting rates and speed**, not just course completion. Phishing simulations should teach, not shame.
