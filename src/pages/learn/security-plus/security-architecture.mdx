## Architecture models

- **On-premises:** you control everything and you're responsible for everything. An **air-gapped** network has no connection to outside networks at all.
- **Cloud:** deployment can be *public*, *private*, *community* or *hybrid* (a mix). **Multicloud** spreads work across more than one provider. **Responsibility** is shared: the provider secures what it runs, and you still own your users, data and settings.
- **Serverless:** you supply code and the provider runs everything under it.
- **Microservices:** an app split into small services that talk to each other, which means more connections to secure.
- **Segmentation:** *logical* (separate networks in software, like VLANs) or *physical* (separate hardware).
- **Infrastructure as Code:** environments defined in version-controlled files, so they're repeatable and reviewable.
- **Weigh the trade-offs:** availability, resilience, cost, scalability, ease of recovery, power and compute needs, usability, proprietary vs. open source, and **data sovereignty** (which country's laws apply to the data).

## Securing the infrastructure

Plan trust boundaries and allow only the traffic you need. Avoid one big flat network.

- **Placement and zones:** put devices in the right **security zone**, so internet-facing servers are kept apart from internal systems. Keep the **attack surface** small.
- **Zero Trust architecture:** check the user (authentication), the device (managed, healthy, in inventory), and the app before granting access, every time.
- **Secure access:** **VPNs** and tunneling protect remote access. **Out-of-band management** keeps admin access on a separate path. Use secure file transfer and end-to-end encrypted messaging.
- **Security Service Edge (SSE):** cloud-delivered security that sits between users and the apps they use.
- **Identity management:** give accounts least privilege, use **group managed service accounts (gMSAs)** so service passwords rotate automatically, and watch for **privilege creep** (people collecting access they no longer need).
- **Failure modes:** decide whether a failure should lock things down (fail closed) or keep them usable (fail open).

## Data protection

Data needs protection in three states: **at rest** (stored), **in transit** (moving over a network), and **in use** (being processed in memory). It can be *structured* (like a database) or *unstructured* (like documents and email).

- **Classification** labels data by sensitivity (public, confidential, restricted, secret, top secret, critical) and sets the handling rules, including marking and labeling.
- **Encryption** and **hashing** protect data at rest and in transit.
- **Tokenization** swaps a real value for a token that maps back to it in a protected vault.
- **Masking** hides part of a value, like showing only the last four digits. **De-identification** removes what ties data to a person.
- **Roles:** the *owner* is accountable, the *custodian* handles day-to-day protection, the *steward* looks after quality, and the *controller* decides how personal data is used.
- **Location:** **geofencing** and data placement rules control where data can be stored or used.
- **Life cycle:** creation, management, distribution, retention, and disposal.

## Resilience and recovery

- **RTO:** how fast a service must be back. **RPO:** how much data, measured in time, you can afford to lose. **MTTR** is the average time to repair. **MTBF** is the average time between failures.
- **Backups:** set the scope and the retention. **Immutable** backups can't be changed or deleted, which helps against ransomware. **Test your restores**, not just your backups.
- **Sites:** a *hot* site is ready to go, a *warm* site is partly ready, and a *cold* site is just space and power.
- **Redundancy:** load balancing, clustering, autoscaling and high availability. **Platform diversity** (more than one vendor or platform) avoids a single shared weakness.
- **Power:** a UPS covers short outages, a generator covers long ones, and redundant power supplies and surge protectors protect the hardware.
- **Testing:** failover tests, simulations and parallel processing show whether disaster recovery and business continuity plans actually work. **Capacity planning** makes sure you can handle the load.
