Remote Small-Office IT Setup

For small businesses and solo professionals who want the basics done right without hiring an IT department. Everything happens remotely by video call, screen share and written notes, using your accounts so you keep full ownership.

What a setup can include

Pick what you need; we scope it together first.

Business email & domain

Set up Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 on your own domain: mailboxes, aliases like hello@ or billing@, shared calendars and a clean admin account that you own.

Email authentication

Publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC records so your mail is more likely to land in inboxes and harder to spoof. Start DMARC in monitor mode, then tighten it once reports look clean.

Wi-Fi & router security walkthrough

A guided video session on your own router: firmware updates, strong admin and Wi-Fi passwords, WPA2/WPA3, a separate guest network and turning off features you don't use.

Backups

A simple 3-2-1 plan for the files that matter: what gets backed up, where, how often, and a test restore so you know it works.

Password manager rollout

Pick a business password manager, set up shared vaults, turn on two-factor sign-in for key accounts and give everyone a short how-to.

Device setup checklists

A written checklist for new and departing laptops and phones: updates, disk encryption, screen lock, accounts in and out. Yours to reuse.

How a setup works

Who it's for, the steps, what you provide and what affects the price.

Who it's for

For small offices and solo pros, roughly 1 to 10 people, whose email, passwords and backups grew up by accident. It gets the basics set up properly, on accounts you own, without hiring an IT department.

How it works

  1. Scoping call: A short video call to list what you have today and pick what is in scope. You get a fixed quote before any work starts.
  2. Setup sessions: I do the work remotely by screen share and in the admin consoles you give me access to. Some steps, like DNS changes, can take a day or two to settle.
  3. Check it works: We send test emails, check the authentication records, try signing in with two-factor and do a test restore from backup.
  4. Walkthrough and handoff: A short walkthrough of what changed, the written notes and checklists, and a review that you hold every admin login.

What you provide

  • Admin access to your domain registrar or DNS host, and to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 if you already use one
  • A list of the people, mailboxes and devices to include
  • Someone at the office who can join a video call and follow along on the router and devices

Works with

Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, common business password managers (for example Bitwarden or 1Password), mainstream cloud backup tools, and consumer or prosumer routers you already own. Other tools are checked on the scoping call.

Typical turnaround

Usually 1 to 2 weeks from the scoping call, depending on scope and how quickly sessions can be booked. Your quote gives the plan.

What affects the final price

  • How many people, mailboxes and devices are included
  • Moving existing email from another provider
  • How many of the setup items you need
  • Extra domains, or cleanup of an old or messy setup

Software subscriptions (for example Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or a password manager) are billed to you directly by those providers and are not part of this price.

For offices that already have their basics set up and want someone to email when a small change or question comes up, without a big IT contract.

What you provide

Works with

What affects the final price

  • How many people are covered
  • How many requests are included each month
  • The response window you choose

No 24/7, emergency or onsite cover. Bigger projects are scoped and quoted separately.

Scope and exclusions

In scope

  • Small offices and solo pros, roughly 1–10 people
  • Remote work only: video call, screen share, email
  • Mainstream cloud tools: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, common password managers and backup tools
  • Consumer and prosumer routers and Wi-Fi gear you already own

Not included

  • Onsite visits or hardware installs
  • Government systems or contracts
  • Regulated data handling or compliance work (for example HIPAA or PCI)
  • 24/7, emergency or after-hours response
  • Guarantees of security, uptime or deliverability

If something's outside this scope, I'll say so on the scoping call and point you to a better fit.
